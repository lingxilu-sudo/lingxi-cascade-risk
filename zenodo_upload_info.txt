## Title
A Physics-Based Monte Carlo Framework for Space Debris Cascade Risk and Insurance Pricing

## Authors
Lu, Lingxi (Independent Researcher)

## Version
2.0

## Description
This working paper presents a comprehensive framework for quantifying space debris cascade risk and its implications for the satellite insurance market. The model integrates collision physics (Kessler-Flournoy collision rates, NASA Standard Breakup Model) with actuarial science (Solvency II premium decomposition, Gaussian Copula correlation modeling, Extreme Value Theory tail estimation) to provide physics-driven insurance pricing.

**Key Findings:**
- Debris grows 41× over 50 years under business-as-usual conditions (36,500 to 1.51M objects)
- Historical pricing underestimates risk by 16.5× (cascade-adjusted premium: $2,058M/year vs historical $125M/year)
- Tail risk is substantial: CVaR(95%) ($1,565M) exceeds expected loss ($921M) by 70%
- The framework reveals an unfunded liability of $1,933M/year in the global space insurance market

**Methodology:**
- Monte Carlo simulation (1,000 paths, 50-year horizon)
- Zero free parameters in physics layer (all parameters from NASA/IADC literature)
- Gaussian Copula for correlated satellite losses
- EVT/GPD for extreme tail estimation
- Solvency II compliant premium decomposition

**Code:** Python package `cascade_risk/` with full test suite (25 tests passing)
