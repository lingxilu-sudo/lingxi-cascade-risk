## Title
A Physics-Based Monte Carlo Framework for Space Debris Cascade Risk and Insurance Pricing

## Authors
Lu, Lingxi (Independent Researcher)

## Version
4.0

## Description
The commercial space insurance market, valued at approximately $1.2 billion in 2025, prices satellite risk using historical launch failure rates. No existing actuarial model accounts for cascade risk — the chain-reaction scenario in which one collision generates debris that triggers further collisions, creating correlated losses across an insurance portfolio. I present a coupled physics-actuarial model that simulates debris cascade dynamics in Low Earth Orbit (LEO) using Monte Carlo methods driven by first-principles collision physics, and translates the resulting loss distributions into risk-adjusted insurance premiums via Value at Risk (VaR) and Conditional VaR (CVaR).

My baseline simulation at 800 km altitude demonstrates that debris populations grow from 36,500 to ~1.51 million objects over 50 years under business-as-usual conditions, with cascade-adjusted premiums 16.5x higher than historical-loss pricing ($2,058M vs. $125M per year for a 500-satellite portfolio). The framework is calibrated against three documented collision events (Iridium 33 x Cosmos 2251, Fengyun-1C, Kosmos 1408) and decomposes premiums into expected loss plus risk, capital, and expense loadings following the Solvency II framework.

Key Findings:
- Debris grows 41x over 50 years under business-as-usual conditions (36,500 to 1.51M objects)
- Cascade-adjusted premiums are 16.5x higher than a 0.5% in-orbit failure benchmark ($2,058M vs. $125M/year)
- Tail risk is substantial: CVaR(95%) ($1,565M) exceeds expected loss ($921M) by 70%
- The framework reveals an unfunded liability of $1,933M/year in the global space insurance market

Methodology:
- Monte Carlo simulation (1,000 paths, 50-year horizon)
- Collision rates from first-principles orbital mechanics (Kessler-Flournoy kinetics)
- Asymmetric fragment yields: debris-satellite vs. debris-debris collisions
- Event-anchored log-normal yield distributions calibrated to three real collision events
- Solvency-II-style premium decomposition (Expected Loss + Risk Loading + Capital Charge + Expense Loading)

Code: Python package cascade_risk/ with full test suite (43 tests passing)
